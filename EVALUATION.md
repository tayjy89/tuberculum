# Tuberculum evaluation record — 2.2

The learner experience uses a matched 10-item knowledge check before and after the course, with six identical task-specific confidence ratings. The 30-decision final assessment remains a separate form. Baseline answers are withheld until the after-course check; final-assessment feedback is part of the learning intervention. Repeat exposure to the matched items can contribute to improvement. There is no validated pass standard or causal effectiveness claim.

EVAL-1.0 covers interpretation (2), disease evaluation/exclusion (2), prescribing (2), monitoring/completion (2), and shared decisions/coordination (2). Single-choice items score 1 for the keyed answer and 0 otherwise; “I am not sure” is available. Confidence is ordinal, from 1 (not at all) to 5 (extremely); “prefer not to rate” is stored as null. Feedback is optional and does not affect certificate access.

Responses and drafts are stored only in the browser. Nothing is sent centrally. Export is a voluntary JSON download with a randomly assigned participant ID, schema/course/form versions, timestamps, raw responses, scoring metadata, reference-use flag, baseline timing flag, confidence, optional feedback, and final-assessment attempts. Certificate names are not stored or exported. Free text can identify a person despite the on-screen request to omit identifying details; exports should be treated accordingly. Clearing the learning record clears the evaluation record too.

The first committed before and after records are retained; these are not overwritten by reassessment. Final-assessment attempts are retained separately. Local storage is editable by the learner, can be cleared, and does not support verified identity or institutional certification. The sample certificate is deliberately a mock-up and has no pass/fail or accreditation claim.

Before using these data for publication, agree the study population and recruitment, prospective protocol, ethics/research-governance determination, participant information/consent where applicable, secure collection destination, retention/access policy, and analysis plan. Prefer paired knowledge scores as the primary educational outcome, with confidence by domain and acceptability as secondary outcomes. Report missing baselines, late baselines, reference use, attrition, repeat attempts, and test-retest limitations. Consider a delayed assessment with fresh cases for retention and transfer. Obtain expert item review and pilot response-process evidence; investigate difficulty, discrimination, distractor performance and ambiguity before stronger assessment claims.

Clinical content, assessment forms and scoring keys should be versioned together. Preserve each released form and key for historical analysis. New standard setting and an equivalent reassessment form are required before an assessment pass is used as a qualification criterion. No workplace assessment is part of this course.
